# Rules at the table

## Your first five minutes

1. Select up to four resource points. Oil/gas feed energy; agriculture feeds people.
   Iron/timber sustain infrastructure. Rare earths support military technology; gold earns reserves.
2. Check Economy for shortages and liabilities. The first shortages emerge as starting stocks run out.
3. Find another Republic with the resource you need. Talk first; open their public profile and propose trade.
4. Adjust Cabinet policies only when you can afford the delayed spending. Every bill takes three months.
5. Watch World Wire, the Assembly and cabinet crises. Every president stays in the game until the end.

## Units and timing

Money is **billions**. Population is **millions**. Resource amounts are abstract strategic units,
not barrels/tonnes. Each session contains 240 automatically advancing months starting January 2030.
A month lasts 11.25 / 15 / 22.5 seconds in a 45 / 60 / 90 minute game, plus small scheduling overhead.
The clock pauses during server downtime and resumes after restart; offline players' Republics continue
while the server runs. Elections occur every 48 months. Ordinary crises arrive every 18 months.

## Economy and political pressure

Education, infrastructure, regional development, energy, food, tax, sanctions, trade pacts and war
shape monthly GDP growth. Tax funds the treasury; policy budgets, debt interest and war drain it.
A treasury shortfall automatically increases debt. High debt feeds inflation and damages scoring.
Employment, healthcare, inflation, food and social spending shape approval, which feeds stability.
Population responds to healthcare and war casualties. State development affects national growth;
state unemployment reflects national conditions and regional development. Political stability also
changes military effectiveness. The House is elected from approval, stability and inflation.

Bills cost five political capital with a majority and twelve without. Military intervention costs
$3B and fifteen capital, or $5B and thirty under an opposition House. Political capital regenerates
more slowly with poor approval. Protests reduce it further. You always remain President.

Monthly consumption is 1.7 oil/gas combined, 1.4 agriculture, 0.7 iron/timber combined and up to
0.2 rare earths. Selected specializations produce monthly stocks, aided by infrastructure and
regional industry. Gold can generate monthly export reserves. Inventories cap at 240 during production.

## Agreements

Trade amounts are **annual**, delivered as twelve equal monthly transfers. The first delivery
happens immediately on acceptance; the rest continue for the selected two, five or ten years.
No resources are conjured on agreement. Either party lacking payment/resources breaks delivery.
Insufficient payment damages the defaulting party's reputation; both economies experience disruption.
A deliberate break costs twelve reputation, four influence and one percent GDP. Offers expire after
twelve months. Recipients may accept, reject or counter; a counter replaces the original pending offer.

Trade pacts improve member growth; defence pacts notify allies when invaded. Assistance remains a
choice. Leaving a pact costs ten reputation and three influence, and records a betrayal.
Bilateral loans are funded by the lender on acceptance and repay 110% over 24 monthly instalments.
Arrears remain payable and erode the borrower's reputation; accepting a loan does not create money.
Sanctions cost $0.5B, slightly damage the issuer's GDP, suppress the target's growth, block bilateral
trade and expire in 24 months.

The Global Development Bank lends $5B while debt is below GDP and improves infrastructure six
months later. The Monetary Authority offers emergency reserves with debt, tax and spending conditions.
The World Trade Council opens a $1B arbitration vote to lift sanctions. World Assembly motions need
more than half of all Republics in favour; abstention cannot turn a minority into a majority.

## Conflict

An ultimatum costs political capital. An invasion runs for six months and opens a sanction vote if
the Assembly floor is free. GDP slows, population falls, military strength and infrastructure degrade.
Outside Republics can make one military or $2B financial commitment per war, to either side.
Combat combines military, defence spending, GDP, education, domestic stability, outside assistance,
a defender geography advantage and seeded uncertainty. The loser pays bounded reparations
(up to $2B and at most 20% of reserves). Nobody loses their Republic or seat.

## Crisis choices

Public intervention spends $2B for a delayed service improvement and approval recovery.
Foreign appeal costs approval and publicly asks other players for help; it opens an aid vote if free.
Private intervention recovers services but creates delayed inflation and an immediate approval cost.
Ignoring a crisis costs approval and infrastructure. These effects are queued and applied by the server.

## Victory

The final score is the sum of five categories, each capped at 200:

- Economy: output, reserves, debt and fulfilled deliveries.
- Prosperity: employment, education, health and output per citizen.
- Influence: diplomatic standing, reputation and active pacts.
- Strength: infrastructure, energy security and military.
- Stability: approval, stability and House support.

The maximum score is 1,000. The results include category winners, military/diplomatic awards,
debt and diplomacy statistics. The host can start a rematch with the same seats and fresh economies.
Exact tuning is in the small, transport-independent engine modules.
