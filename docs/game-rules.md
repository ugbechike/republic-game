# Rules at the table

## Your first five minutes

1. Join the World Draft. Each President claims three advantages in a timed snake order.
   Deposits have limited availability and differing quality; every claim is public.
   Every Republic starts from the same baseline, with resource-specific advantages.
2. Watch Presidential Briefings. Ministers identify genuine shortages, required reserves,
   remaining time and Republics that produce a surplus.
3. Speak to another President, then request a bilateral meeting to record the terms.
   Either participant can edit the shared table before a proposal is submitted. The other
   President accepts, rejects or counters; both sign before an agreement takes effect.
   Private chat is optional. Nobody buys from another human automatically.
4. Open Economy to inspect reserve runway, monthly flows, charts and explanations.
5. Resolve the urgent need, then negotiate recurring supply before the next shortage.

## Units, clocks and privacy

Money is fictional game currency in **billions**; population is **millions**. Commodities
use abstract strategic units, not barrels or tonnes. A session contains 240 months from
January 2030. Each month lasts 11.25 / 15 / 22.5 seconds in a 45 / 60 / 90 minute session,
plus scheduling overhead. Elections occur every 48 months.

Server downtime and everyone disconnecting pause months, briefing deadlines and world crises.
An offline Republic continues while any human remains connected. Reconnecting restores the
saved seat and schedules a fresh monthly interval; missed offline time is not simulated.
Development time acceleration also accelerates deadlines.

Your stocks, internal needs and metric explanations are private. Public profiles include
estimated production surplus, credit and existing public metrics. Proposal reasons are optional
and shared only with the counterpart. Announcements and assistance requests may be public.

## Resources and shortages

Five-point budget: oil 2, gas 2, gold 2, rare earths 3, iron 1, farmland 1, timber 1,
coastline 2, rivers 1, uranium 3. Coastline and rivers are geography, not tradeable inventory.
No legal selection produces every essential input. Coastlines add fisheries and port income;
rivers add food and reduce fuel requirements; uranium reduces oil/gas dependence but has costs.

For a population of 10M, baseline monthly demand is oil 0.85, gas 0.70, food 1.40,
iron 0.80, timber 0.30 and rare earths 0.18. Uranium demand is 0.04 only for uranium
producers or an energy programme of at least 12. Population and geography modify demand.
Gold has no domestic consumption. Selected industries produce stocks, aided by regional
industry and infrastructure. Non-owned essentials begin with five months of reserves.

The ledger records actual production, consumption, imports, exports, reserves and runway.
Small external exports remove commodities from inventory and earn revenue; they do not
replace human trade. Production inventories cap at 240. Extraction incurs operating costs.

An unmet monthly requirement increases shortage duration. Every two shortage months moves
through prices rising, service interruptions, output falling, jobs at risk and lost approval.
Penalties are gradual. Covered demand reduces accumulated shortage duration by two months
per tick; residual effects fade before disappearing. One shipment buys time; recurring supply
can cover the structural deficit. Other unresolved resources still affect the economy.

## Ministers and deadlines

The director considers reserves, actual flows, potential suppliers, active contracts, alliances,
public services, debt exposure, world conditions, regional infrastructure, recent issues and game
phase. It prioritizes problems that humans can help solve. It never scripts human responses.

First warnings can appear after 30–45 seconds. Subsequent domestic issues are paced about
3.5 minutes early, three minutes in midgame and 2.5 minutes late, with at most one ordinary
open briefing per Republic. Multiplayer offers and world events have their own notifications.

Shortage, treasury and security briefings allow three active minutes; floods allow four.
A shortage asks for eight months of reserves or enough delivered recurring supply. Treasury
pressure eases at $5B. Flood reconstruction consumes $1.8B and 2.4 iron; missing the deadline
costs $4.2B GDP and regional/national infrastructure. Other missed deadlines cost two approval
while the underlying economic problem continues.

You can request human assistance, delay once for one minute at a cost of one approval,
seek $1B Assembly mediation, or negotiate. Emergency procurement costs $3B and two approval
for four months of one input, once per briefing. It is a temporary fallback when suppliers
will not trade. Bank eligibility and terms are shown before taking a facility.

## Trade, loans and institutions

A package can combine cash and multiple commodities, delivered immediately or as annual
quantities split into twelve monthly transfers. Recurring terms run one to ten years and can
start now or after three, six or twelve months. Immediate items transfer once on acceptance.
Both sides must fund their own entire delivery before either receives anything. Future
obligations are checked again at settlement; no money or resources are conjured.

An immediate-only exchange completes on acceptance. Recurring contracts continue until term
end or failure. Countering replaces the original pending proposal; unanswered offers expire
after twelve months. A deliberate break costs twelve reputation, four influence and 1% GDP.
Insufficient reserves or sanctions stop the exchange atomically, hurt both economies and
penalize an unfunded party's reputation.

Bilateral loans specify principal, total repayment, term and monthly or maturity-only repayment.
The lender funds acceptance. Six missed instalments or unpaid maturity triggers default:
unpaid debt remains, reputation and relationships fall, and credit limits future principal.
Cash now for recurring resources can be negotiated as a trade package instead.

The Global Development Bank's emergency facility requires treasury below $5B. It supplies $5B
with 7% total interest, repaid over five years. Accepting immediately cuts each spending budget
by at least 8%, raises corporate tax by up to three points (40% cap), cancels incompatible
pending bills, binds spending ceilings/tax floor, and suspends new infrastructure construction
for 24 months. Conditions remain until the five-year term and repayment are complete.
Ordinary development loans retain debt eligibility and are unavailable during this facility.

## Economic policy and explanations

Education, infrastructure, regional development, energy, food, tax, sanctions, trade pacts and
war shape GDP. Tax and external exports fund treasury; spending, extraction, debt service
and war drain it. Treasury shortfalls add debt. Employment, inflation, healthcare, food and
social spending affect approval and stability. Population responds to healthcare and war.

Bills take three months and cost five political capital with a majority, twelve without.
The House is elected from approval, stability and inflation; you always remain President.

Economy charts cover GDP, fiscal revenue/expenditure, debt, inflation, unemployment,
energy/food delivered versus demand, trade cash balance and selected commodity flow history.
Event markers and a text history connect deals, briefings and crises with outcomes. Metric
breakdowns explain the latest month's changes; overlapping causes are not counted twice.

## World crises, sanctions and conflict

A world crisis occurs about every twelve active minutes and lasts three minutes:

- Energy: oil/gas output −25%, external export price +50%.
- Food: harvest/fishery output −25%, external food export price +40%.
- Commodity crash: mining external export prices −40%.
- Recession: external export demand −25%, higher debt servicing; trade pacts cushion output.

Human prices remain negotiable. Fixed-price contracts do not automatically reprice.
Producers may gain bargaining power while importers lose reserve runway.

Trade sanctions block bilateral exchanges; resource embargoes block packages involving the
selected commodity; financial sanctions block cash and loans; condemnation reduces reputation.
Measures last 24 months, cost $0.5B and some issuer output. Lost imports can hurt the issuer.
Coordinated sanctions can be proposed to the Assembly for $1B. Votes allow a 30-second
active discussion window, then Yes/No/Abstain; passage needs more than half of all Republics.

Persistent scarcity and repeated refused offers can prompt security advice after the opening
phase. Continue negotiating, seek mediation, use alternative procurement, organize pressure,
or consider force. War is never automatic. Trade and defence pacts remain voluntary.

An ultimatum costs capital. Invasion costs $3B/15 capital, or $5B/30 under an opposition House,
and runs for six months. The defender can broadcast a help request. Other Republics can offer
military or $2B financial support to either side, discuss terms, or remain neutral. Combat
accounts for military, defence, GDP, education, stability, assistance, geography and seeded
uncertainty. Losses and reparations are bounded; nobody loses their Republic or seat.

## Victory and tuning

Five categories each contribute at most 200 points: economy, prosperity, influence, strength
and stability. Results include awards, debt and diplomacy statistics. The host can rematch
with the same seats and fresh economies. Balance numbers are explicit V0 tuning parameters,
not economic forecasts. The social pacing still needs a sustained playtest with friends.

## Presidential workspace

The desk folder opens prioritized correspondence; the phone opens bilateral meetings;
the window visits your Republic; the wall map opens the World. Cabinet and economic
reports remain available through objects in the office. Returning from a briefing's
city visit restores that document. The calendar highlights the nearest decisions.

Governors request full or partial reimbursements, or can be refused. Businesses offer
factory support or temporary incentives. Accepted construction persists in the landscape
and takes time to produce returns. Funded flood reconstruction gradually clears the
visible damage; an expired briefing does not magically repair the region.

Meeting proposals lock the shared terms. Countering reopens editing and clears previous
signatures. Both Presidents sign the same revision; the second signature validates and
executes the exchange atomically. An unaffordable exchange leaves both Republics untouched.
Reloading preserves private chat, proposed terms and existing signatures. Signed records
remain available in Meetings. Existing pacts, loans and institutional actions are available
through Foreign Policy; they do not yet use the shared signing table.
